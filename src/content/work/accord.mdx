## Start with the company directory

The design starts with a common task. Someone looks up a company, scans its record for context, then may need to find a related task, meeting, or note. The directory gives the record most of the screen and keeps those other destinations within reach.

## Give the current task more space

A directory needs room for detailed records. Accord puts actions that affect the table beside it and places workspace-wide controls in the sidebar. Someone comparing companies can focus on the rows and still see where they are in the workspace.

## Group records and tools

The sidebar groups Companies, People, and Opportunities under Records. Tasks, Meetings, and Notes sit under Tools. A filled selection connects Companies in the sidebar to the table on screen.

Search, Home, and Inbox sit above both groups. Integrations and Help & Support sit near the bottom, with Partners under List. These destinations give the workspace its shape. The case study focuses on the Companies view.

## Make company records easy to scan

Search companies, Filter, and Display sit above the rows in All Companies. New sits in the page header. The mockups show where these actions start. A future prototype could explore search results and company details.

Names and domains give people two ways to recognize a company. Consistent columns make it easier to compare employee counts and revenue across rows. Category tags carry text labels, with color as a second cue.

Seventeen sample records fit on screen. That density helps with comparison, though long addresses and some groups of tags already reach the edges of their columns. The records are sample data.

## Keep workspace actions together

The selector shows the current workspace at the top of the sidebar. Open it to see Accord and My Space, with a check beside the active workspace. Invite people, Settings, Theme, and Log out sit below the workspace choices.

Grouping these actions frees up space around the directory. People need to open the selector to find them, so discovering Invite people and Settings is worth testing in a prototype.

## Keep the hierarchy in both appearances

The Light and Dark mockups keep the selected Companies item, table columns, and menu groups in the same positions. The Theme submenu shows three choices for a future implementation. Theme switching and saved preferences still need to be defined.

## Use the same mark at both scales

The geometric mark from the opening artwork reappears beside Accord in the workspace selector. Its silhouette carries the identity into the interface, where the icon has to work at a much smaller size.

## What to test next

An interactive prototype would let us check whether people can do the following.

- Find a named company and compare its details with another record.
- Identify a company’s categories from the tag labels.
- Locate the current workspace, Invite people, and Settings.
- Read long addresses and category groups at narrower widths.
