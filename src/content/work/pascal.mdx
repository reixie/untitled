## Design around the note

Pascal starts with a personal writing task. Someone opens a draft, adds to it, and switches to a reference before returning to the draft. The editor gives the note most of the window, while the sidebar and tabs keep those moves close at hand.

## Find a note and return to it

The sidebar separates collection destinations such as Favorites and Archives from individual notes. Tabs above the editor give open notes a predictable place to return to. The active note is highlighted in both places, so its location in the collection stays visible while someone writes.

Settings sits at the bottom of the sidebar, away from the notes someone opens every day. Keeping the sidebar in view uses horizontal space but makes it easier to see where a note belongs.

## Make structured notes easy to read

The sample Research Brief brings links, a method, an evidence table, and tasks into one Markdown note. Headings and spacing separate those sections. Aligned table columns support comparison, while checkboxes distinguish tasks from the rest of the note.

## Group formatting options by purpose

The formatting menu gives each kind of change a place. Style sets heading levels, Blocks organizes text into structures such as lists, and Media adds images, bookmarks, or files. The groups shorten the scan once someone opens the menu. Finding the menu itself is a task to test.

## Search for notes and commands together

The command palette uses one search field for Notes and Actions. Notes keep the names and icons people already see in the sidebar. The Actions group gives workspace commands their own place in the results.

The mockups show the palette and tabs. An interactive walkthrough could test the handoff between them by asking someone to open Research Brief from the palette, switch to Writing Ideas, then return to Research Brief.

## Keep personal work easy to recognize

My Space names the personal workspace shown here. Team collaboration is part of the concept. A future prototype should show how people recognize a shared note and return to their personal workspace.

## Keep orientation cues in Light and Dark

Both appearances use surface contrast to separate navigation from the editor and filled selections to mark the open note. The palette keeps its groups and actions in the same order. Muted borders and secondary labels are the details to check for readability in both appearances.

## What to test next

Use an interactive prototype to check whether people can do the following.

- Find and reopen a note through the command palette.
- Switch between tabs and identify the active note.
- Find the formatting menu and insert a checklist.
- Open the Markdown source and see how the checklist is stored.
- Tell whether a note is personal or shared.
